# Introduction

This guideline sets out an ethical framework and practical guidance for the responsible and trustworthy development and use of AI in Earth observation (EO), covering the whole lifecycle from data acquisition to model deployment and decision-making. It addresses the developers of foundation models (FMs) and agents, data and platform providers, the teams that apply models in operational or research use cases, and the bodies that review and oversee this work. It is a living document that is revised as methods, regulation and experience develop. The guideline was first produced as the Ethics and Responsible AI Guidelines (D1.3) of the PROTEUS project; the project context, the roles of the PROTEUS partners and the way the project applies the guideline are described in Annexes F to L.

## Purpose and Scope

This subsection states what the guideline is for and where it applies. Its purpose is to give everyone who develops or uses AI for EO a common, practical basis for building responsible AI into foundation models and the services built on them, and to provide evidence that can be reviewed and compared across applications. It covers datasets, pretrained and fine-tuned foundation models, hybrid AI-physics models and agentic components, whether developed in-house or adopted from third parties, and it applies at every lifecycle stage: data collection, model training, evaluation and reporting, and deployment. It names the roles it addresses (foundation model developers, data and platform providers, use case leads, and governance and review bodies) and says how it treats proprietary or third-party models, which are assessed on documented behaviour and available evidence rather than on access to their internals. It also states which requirements are mandatory, which are recommended, and which depend on tools still in development. How the guideline relates to the PROTEUS Grant Agreement, Consortium Agreement and Data Management Plan is described in Annexes F and K.

## How to Use This Guideline

This subsection tells each reader where to start and what to do. It gives reading paths by role: a foundation model developer goes first to the lifecycle checkpoints, the relevant principles and the risk register, then to the strategies for the methods and the metadata schema for the documentation to produce; a use case lead goes to the principles, the compliance assessment process and the use case alignment; a data or platform provider goes to the privacy, geoprivacy and metadata requirements. It explains the wording used for requirements (must, should, may), how requirements are numbered so that they can be cited in the compliance card, and how the sections relate: principles say what is required, the risk register says why, the strategies say how, and the schema and compliance process define the evidence to submit and when. It also points to the annexes (checklist, JSON schema, standards crosswalk, glossary, and the PROTEUS annexes), explains how to raise questions and request exceptions, and explains how readers can propose changes for the next version.

## Roles and Responsibilities

This subsection defines who does what, using generic roles that any project or organisation can assign. Foundation model developers, meaning those who pretrain or fine-tune models, are responsible for meeting the requirements at each lifecycle stage and for submitting the compliance card and metadata. Data and platform providers are responsible for data documentation, privacy and geoprivacy screening, and the metadata infrastructure. Use case leads and deployers apply the guideline to their application, take part in its evaluation and report operational concerns. A responsible AI reviewer maintains the guideline, reviews submissions, advises developers and escalates unresolved findings. An independent advisory or ethics body reviews responsible AI integration and ethical concerns. The subsection includes a table that assigns each requirement area to a role; the decision rights at release are defined in the governance section. Annex F maps these roles to the PROTEUS partners.
