# Introduction

This guideline is the first version of the Ethics and Responsible AI Guidelines (D1.3) of PROTEUS, produced under the Responsible AI4EO, ethics and standards task (T1.4 & T2.4) led by HZDR. It sets out the ethical framework and initial guidelines for the responsible and trustworthy development of AI in the project, covering the whole lifecycle from data acquisition to model deployment and decision-making. The guideline is public and addresses all consortium partners, in particular the developers of foundation models (FMs), the leads of the seven use cases (UC1 to UC7), the data and platform teams, and the External Advisory Board. It is a living document: this version (M10) is revised into D2.2 (M36) using lessons from the use case evaluations and the two prototype cycles, and it is linked to the technical work on responsible, secure and uncertainty-aware FMs (T3.4 & T4.4, Key Result KR06).

## Purpose and Scope

This subsection states what the guideline is for and where it applies. Its purpose is to give every partner a common, practical basis for building responsible AI into Copernicus foundation models and the services built on them, and to provide the evidence base for the evaluation of KR06 across the seven use cases. It covers the datasets, pretrained and fine-tuned foundation models, hybrid AI-physics models and agentic components that are developed or adopted in PROTEUS, and it applies at every lifecycle stage: data collection, model training, evaluation and reporting, and deployment. It names the roles it addresses (foundation model developers, data and platform providers, use case leads, and project governance) and says how it treats models that partners bring as background IP, which are assessed on documented behaviour and available evidence rather than on access to their internals. It also states which requirements are mandatory, which are recommended, and which are still in development in T3.4 & T4.4, and how the guideline relates to the Grant Agreement, the Consortium Agreement and the Data Management Plan.

## How to Use This Guideline

This subsection tells each reader where to start and what to do. It gives reading paths by role: a foundation model developer goes first to the lifecycle checkpoints, the relevant principles and the risk register, then to the strategies for the methods and the metadata schema for the documentation to produce; a use case lead goes to the principles, the compliance assessment process and the use case alignment; a data or platform provider goes to the privacy, geoprivacy and metadata requirements. It explains the wording used for requirements (must, should, may), how requirements are numbered so that they can be cited in the compliance card, and how the sections relate: principles say what is required, the risk register says why, the strategies say how, and the schema and compliance process define the evidence to submit and when. It also points to the annexes (checklist, JSON schema, standards crosswalk, glossary), gives the contact for questions and exceptions, and explains how partners can propose changes for the next version.

## Roles and Responsibilities

This subsection defines who does what. Foundation model developers, meaning the partners that pretrain or fine-tune models, are responsible for meeting the requirements at each lifecycle stage and for submitting the compliance card and metadata. Data and platform providers are responsible for data documentation, privacy and geoprivacy screening, and the metadata infrastructure (WP9, WP10 and SERCO). Use case leads (UC1 to UC7) apply the guideline to their application, take part in the KR06 evaluation and report operational concerns. The RAI lead at HZDR maintains the guideline, reviews submissions, advises partners and escalates unresolved findings. The External Advisory Board reviews responsible AI integration and ethical concerns. The subsection includes a table that assigns each requirement area to a role and states the decision rights at release.
